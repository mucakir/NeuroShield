      Next step: a small language model on board planned, not yet connected

      Today the answers above are assembled by rules from the crew member's own data. The plan is to let a small language model run on the spacecraft's own computer (on-premise), next to NeuroShield, and write these answers in natural language.

      Works without EarthAt Mars a question to the ground takes up to 24 minutes for a reply; an on-board model answers in seconds and keeps working during communication blackouts.
      Health data stays on boardMedical records never leave the vehicle; nothing is sent to a cloud service.
      Simple hardwareA 4-billion-parameter model runs on an ordinary laptop without a GPU, so it fits the power, mass and radiation-hardened computing limits of a spacecraft.
      Reads the whole missionA long context window lets it read a crew member's full logbook at once and explain trends, not just single values.
      Our choice: MedGemma 1.5 4B (Google)open weights · medical
      VersionReleased in January 2026. Built on Gemma 3, focused on medicine, open weights.
      Hardware4 billion parameters. About 3.3 GB with Q4 quantisation in Ollama, so it runs on an ordinary laptop with 8 GB RAM and no GPU. On a CPU alone it is somewhat slow, but fast enough for answers of a few sentences.
      CapabilityAbout 69 % on the MedQA medical question-answering benchmark. Clearly better than the previous version at answering questions about patient records. A 128K-token context window is enough to read an astronaut's 60-day logbook in one go.
      LicenceDistributed under the Health AI Developer Foundations terms and not approved for direct clinical use, so it would explain and suggest, while decisions stay with the Crew Medical Officer and the flight surgeon.

      Health indicators, reference ranges and how they are measured

      How the health score works

      Each indicator gets a sub-score: 100 inside the normal (green) band, falling to 60 at the caution limit and to 0 beyond it. A body-system score blends the average and the worst indicator (40/60) so a single dangerous value is never averaged away. The overall score is the weighted mean of the systems and is capped at 75 while a caution alert is active and at 45 during a critical alert.

      NeuroShield is a decision-support prototype for NASA Space Apps 2026. Crew physiology in the Live Monitor and the logbook is synthetic; the cabin environment, radiation, space weather and blood panels use real NASA and NOAA data (see Datasets). It does not replace the flight surgeon or medical procedures.

      Adding crew members is not available in the demo

      This demo version works with the four built-in crew members (A–D) and their generated mission data, so new profiles cannot be created.

      In the full version each astronaut gets a profile with their own baseline, logbook and live sensor stream, created by the flight surgeon before launch.

      Not available in the demo yet

      Connecting a live device is not possible in the demo version yet. The Live Monitor plays the built-in mission scenarios instead.

      In the full version the NeuroShield wearable streams its readings once per second and the monitor analyses them in real time.

      Connect a live sensor stream

      The NeuroShield wearable (or any source) can push JSON frames once per second over WebSocket, e.g.
      {"HR":72,"SPO2":97,"EEG_FI":1.1,"RSO2":68,"CO2":2100}
